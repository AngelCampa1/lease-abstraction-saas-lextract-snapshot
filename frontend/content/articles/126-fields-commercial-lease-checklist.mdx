A lease abstract is only as good as its coverage. Miss one field and you might overlook a $50,000 escalation clause or a termination right that changes your negotiation leverage entirely.

This checklist covers the 126 fields that a thorough commercial lease abstraction should capture. We organized them by category so you can use this as a reference template whether you abstract leases manually or use an automated tool.

## Parties and Identification (12 fields)

These fields establish who is involved and what property the lease covers.

1. Landlord legal name
2. Landlord contact information
3. Tenant legal name
4. Tenant contact information
5. Guarantor name(s)
6. Guarantor contact information
7. Property name
8. Property address
9. Suite/unit number
10. Lease execution date
11. Lease document type (original, amendment, renewal)
12. Amendment history summary

## Premises Details (8 fields)

Physical space measurements and descriptions that affect everything from rent calculations to CAM allocations.

13. Rentable square footage
14. Usable square footage
15. Common area factor (load factor)
16. Floor(s) occupied
17. Permitted use
18. Exclusive use provisions
19. Parking spaces allocated
20. Storage space included

## Lease Term and Critical Dates (11 fields)

The timeline fields that drive every deadline in your property management system.

21. Lease commencement date
22. Rent commencement date
23. Lease expiration date
24. Original lease term (months)
25. Early occupancy provisions
26. Renewal option count
27. Renewal option term lengths
28. Renewal notice deadline
29. Termination option details
30. Termination notice deadline
31. Holdover provisions and rate

## Base Rent Schedule (9 fields)

The core financial terms that determine what the tenant pays each month.

32. Initial base rent (annual, per SF)
33. Initial base rent (monthly)
34. Rent escalation type (fixed, CPI, market)
35. Fixed escalation percentage/amount
36. CPI escalation formula and cap
37. Market rent adjustment mechanism
38. Abated rent periods (free rent)
39. Abated rent amount/duration
40. Percentage rent trigger and rate

## Operating Expenses and CAM (14 fields)

The section most likely to contain errors and disputes. Capture every detail.

41. Lease structure (NNN, gross, modified gross)
42. Operating expense base year
43. Operating expense stop amount
44. Tenant's pro rata share percentage
45. CAM included expenses list
46. CAM excluded expenses list
47. Controllable expense cap
48. Management fee percentage
49. Management fee cap
50. Capital expenditure treatment
51. Capital expenditure amortization schedule
52. Utility responsibility (landlord vs. tenant)
53. Janitorial responsibility
54. Property tax responsibility

## Insurance Requirements (7 fields)

Coverage minimums the tenant must maintain and evidence regularly.

55. General liability minimum
56. Property/contents insurance minimum
57. Business interruption coverage requirement
58. Professional liability requirement
59. Workers compensation requirement
60. Additional insured endorsement required
61. Certificate delivery deadline

## Tenant Improvements (6 fields)

Buildout terms that affect both the tenant's upfront costs and ongoing obligations.

62. TI allowance amount (total)
63. TI allowance per SF
64. TI delivery timeline
65. TI construction responsibility
66. Excess TI cost responsibility
67. Restoration/surrender requirements

## Security and Guarantees (6 fields)

Financial protections for the landlord that directly affect the tenant's risk.

68. Security deposit amount
69. Security deposit form (cash, LOC, bond)
70. Security deposit reduction schedule
71. Personal guarantee scope
72. Personal guarantee cap amount
73. Personal guarantee burn-off schedule

## Assignment and Subletting (5 fields)

Transfer rights that affect the tenant's flexibility and exit options.

74. Assignment consent requirement
75. Subletting consent requirement
76. Landlord recapture right
77. Profit sharing on assignment/sublease
78. Permitted transfers (affiliates, successors)

## Maintenance and Repairs (5 fields)

Responsibility allocation that drives ongoing operating costs.

79. Tenant maintenance obligations
80. Landlord maintenance obligations
81. HVAC maintenance responsibility
82. Structural repair responsibility
83. Roof and exterior wall responsibility

## Defaults and Remedies (5 fields)

What happens when things go wrong. These fields drive legal strategy.

84. Monetary default cure period
85. Non-monetary default cure period
86. Landlord termination triggers
87. Tenant termination triggers
88. Late payment penalty/interest rate

## Special Provisions (11 fields)

The clauses that differentiate one lease from another and often hold the most value.

89. Right of first refusal (adjacent space)
90. Right of first offer (adjacent space)
91. Expansion option details
92. Contraction option details
93. Co-tenancy clause
94. Exclusive use clause scope
95. Signage rights
96. Rooftop rights (antenna, equipment)
97. Building naming rights
98. Audit rights for operating expenses
99. Subordination, non-disturbance, and attornment (SNDA)

## Using This Checklist

Print this list and compare it to your current abstraction template. If your template is missing fields, you have blind spots in your lease data.

For teams processing more than a handful of leases per month, manual extraction of all 126 fields is a significant time investment. That is exactly the use case where automated extraction tools pay for themselves: they capture every field on every lease, every time, without the fatigue factor that causes human abstractors to skip "less important" fields on lease number 30 of a 50-lease project.

Whatever method you use, completeness matters. A lease abstract that covers 80% of the fields gives you 80% visibility into your obligations. The 20% you miss is where the surprises live.
