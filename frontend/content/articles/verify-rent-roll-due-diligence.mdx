A rent roll is a seller's representation of the income-producing leases at a property. It is also, predictably, optimistic. Sellers have financial incentives to present the most favorable picture of tenant performance, lease terms, and income stability. Buyers who accept a rent roll at face value without verifying it against executed lease documents are accepting the seller's characterization of their own asset.

Rent roll verification is not a distrust exercise - it is a risk quantification exercise. The discrepancies you find determine whether the purchase price is appropriate, what reps and warranties you need, and what risks carry into closing.

## What a Rent Roll Claims vs. What Leases Actually Say

The most revealing comparison is between the rent roll's stated figures and what the executed lease documents actually provide.

**Rent amounts** are the first and most common discrepancy. A rent roll may show current monthly rent without discounting for free rent periods that are still running, or may show a scheduled rent increase that has not yet taken effect as if it were current. Verify each tenant's current rent against the escalation schedule in the executed lease, accounting for where they are in the rent schedule at the proposed closing date.

**Lease expiration dates** are frequently misstated, particularly on leases with multiple renewals that have already been exercised. If a tenant has exercised a five-year renewal, the rent roll should show the new expiration date - but sellers sometimes carry the original expiration and list the renewal as a note rather than incorporating it into the term data.

**Option status** is the most consequential discrepancy category. If a tenant has already exercised a renewal option, that changes the economics significantly. If a tenant has unexercised options, the rent roll should reflect the realistic probability of exercise - but sellers present all options as positive, when a below-market renewal option at the tenant's sole election is actually a risk factor, not a benefit.

**Square footage** discrepancies occur when tenants have expanded or contracted under lease amendments that were not reflected in the original rent roll template. A rent roll showing a tenant at 5,000 square feet when their amendment reduced them to 4,200 square feet overstates the rental income and understates the vacancy.

## The Systematic Verification Process

Efficient rent roll verification requires treating it as a matching exercise between two data sets: the seller's rent roll and the AI extraction from the executed lease documents.

**Step 1: Obtain all executed lease documents.** The data room should contain the original executed lease, all amendments in order, any side letters or operating covenants, and any notice of option exercise. If documents are missing, they must be obtained before verification can proceed. Incomplete document sets are a common source of discrepancies that are actually disclosure failures.

**Step 2: Extract all active leases systematically.** Upload each lease package (base lease plus all amendments) to an extraction tool and receive structured data for all 126 fields. The extraction tells you what the documents actually say, without the seller's editorial framing.

**Step 3: Build a comparison spreadsheet.** For each tenant, place the rent roll figures and the extracted figures side by side across the key verification fields: tenant legal name, premises square footage, lease commencement, rent commencement, expiration date (current term), current annual rent, monthly rent, rent escalation type and next increase date, option status, and CAM structure.

**Step 4: Document every discrepancy.** Flag each cell where the rent roll and extracted data differ. Categorize discrepancies by materiality: a $50 monthly rent difference is a rounding error; a tenant who has already exercised their only renewal option (and the rent roll shows two remaining options) is a material misrepresentation.

## The Most Common Rent Roll Discrepancies

**Free rent not reflected.** Some tenants are still in a free rent period or partial-rent period negotiated as part of the original lease or a recent amendment. The rent roll shows the scheduled rent; the actual cash collection is less. Always ask: what was the actual rent collected over the past 12 months versus what the rent roll projects?

**Tenants in holdover.** When a lease has expired and the tenant is holding over on a month-to-month basis, the holdover status should be disclosed. Holdover tenants can typically leave with 30 days notice, which is very different from a signed 5-year lease.

**Unrecorded amendments.** A verbal agreement or poorly documented side letter may have modified rent, term, or other provisions. The only protection is thorough document requests - but if a side letter exists that the seller does not disclose, it is a rep and warranty issue.

**Misstated pro-rata share.** For multi-tenant properties, the rent roll may show tenant CAM contributions based on lease estimates rather than actual building occupancy and denominator calculations. Verify the building's total rentable area and each tenant's stated pro-rata share.

**Tenant credit issues not disclosed.** A tenant may be current on rent but only because of a landlord-funded payment arrangement after a period of non-payment. Check rent payment history alongside lease verification.

## How to Handle Discrepancies

Not all discrepancies affect purchase price equally. Develop a materiality framework before you start.

Minor discrepancies (rounding, date formatting) get noted and confirmed at closing. Material discrepancies - where the rent roll overstates income, understates risk, or misrepresents option status - require either a price adjustment, a seller's rep and warranty, an indemnification, or a decision to pass on the deal.

For significant discrepancies, the negotiating position is straightforward: the purchase price was based on the rent roll as presented. If the rent roll is materially different from what the leases actually say, the price should be adjusted to reflect the property's actual economics.

Systematic rent roll verification using lease extraction tools converts what is often a 2-3 week manual process into a 2-3 day data exercise, giving acquisition teams more time to analyze the discrepancies they find rather than racing to find them before the due diligence deadline.
